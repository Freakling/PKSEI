# Design decisions

Why the design is the way it is: one line per decision, newest last, never edited. The GDD states the current design; this file says how it got there. A decision that replaces an earlier one says so.

Format: `- YYYY-MM-DD · <decision> · why: <reason> · from: <Q<n> | conversation | playtest file>`

- 2026-10-16 · PKSEI is a rage game: four drunk guys cross a lake to an island on a catamaran made of two aluminium canoes, and the player controls all four at once · why: the human's concept · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Genre is action/arcade; the feelings to hit are rage and mastery, with comedy throughout · why: the human's concept · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Design pillars: Hard but fair; Four bodies, one brain; Drunker is funnier; No death, only setbacks · why: the human picked three from the proposed set and added the fourth, pointing at Getting Over It and A Difficult Game About Climbing · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Nobody dies and there is no game over: a bad stretch drifts the boat back into water already crossed · why: Getting-Over-It-style loss of progress is the rage, not restarting · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Moment to moment is paddle-timing balance: each guy paddles or leans on input and the boat turns and tips from the mix · why: makes coordinating four bodies the control problem itself · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · A run is one lake, one crossing from shore to island; the first version is short and the lake grows later · why: start small, lengthen once the loop is proven · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Win: reach the island with all four guys aboard · why: part of the chosen run option; confirm if a guy overboard should be allowed to count · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · An attempt starts sober-ish at the shore on calm water; drunkenness climbs with time and beers · why: the first stretch teaches control before it degrades · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Platform: web (HTML5 on itch.io), GL Compatibility renderer · why: easy to share playtest builds · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Input: keyboard first; local co-op with one guy per player is a planned mode (exact scheme open, Q1 and Q5) · why: cheapest to build; co-op turns the coordination problem into party chaos · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Canvas 640×360, 16:9, integer scaling, nearest filtering · why: scales cleanly to 720p/1080p/1440p with room for a boat, four guys and hazards ahead · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Camera follows the boat, biased toward the direction of travel · why: hazards visible early serves Hard but fair · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Art direction: top-down, bright pixel art, a bit retro, lots of comedy · why: the human's concept · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Controls: four keys, one per guy, hold to charge and release to stroke, no on-screen meter; local co-op later gives each player two keys, stroke and lean · why: more expressive than a tap and gives drunkenness a second thing to corrupt; the human reserved the two-key scheme for co-op · from: Q1
- 2026-10-16 · The catamaran never capsizes; a wobble is cosmetic · why: it is a catamaran · from: Q2
- 2026-10-16 · The setback is the Rescue: a hazard knocks a beer overboard, a guy jumps in after it, the boat drifts with three paddlers until it reaches him; no calm areas stop the drift (replaces the earlier "drifts back into water already crossed" wording, which it makes concrete) · why: the loss is proportional to the recovery with fewer paddlers, and the rescue is the comedy · from: Q2
- 2026-10-16 · A constant current runs from the island toward the shore at all times · why: the game's gravity; keeps the pressure on without timers · from: Q2
- 2026-10-16 · A rescued guy drinks the saved beer on the spot · why: every setback makes the next stretch harder, the sunk-cost loop in beer form · from: Q2
- 2026-10-16 · Drunkenness degrades control as lag and missed strokes rising with the level, plus telegraphed antics at thresholds (stand up, drop the paddle, nod off); drinking is time-based plus rescued beers, no pickups in the first version · why: chaos in the bodies, not in the input; wind-ups keep it fair · from: Q4
- 2026-10-16 · Scrambled keys only at the top drunkenness tier, announced with a wind-up, shown next to the guy and steady until he sobers or is set back · why: the human wanted scrambling; the announced, steady form keeps the pillar Hard but fair · from: Q4
- 2026-10-16 · Seats matter: all four seats give the same forward push, back seats do most of the steering (meant to feel like 80/20 back/front); a left stroke turns the boat right and a right stroke left, like an outboard at the stern · why: the human's call; makes who sits where part of the coordination problem · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Keys: Q left front, A left back, I right front, K right back; left hand drives the left hull, right hand the right · why: the human's call; one hand per hull · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Stamina: strokes burn it in proportion to power, it recovers while resting; a burnt-out guy takes a break of a few seconds with no strokes, fast recovery and faster drinking; random breaks can come earlier, likelier the more stamina is burnt; every break is telegraphed a second or two ahead · why: the human's call; makes power-versus-stamina a four-way trade-off, and the warning keeps it fair · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · No limit on guys in the water: every beer overboard sends one more; the cascade is intended, one gone much harder, two near impossible, all four and the boat drifts home · why: the human's call; the setback size follows from the player's own recovery · from: Q6
- 2026-10-16 · The beer drifts with the current; the swimmer swims to it, then floats drifting with the current and drinking it, and never swims back on his own (replaces "drinks the saved beer on the spot": he drinks it in the water, so floating longer costs more) · why: the human's call; the longer the rescue the drunker he comes back · from: Q6
- 2026-10-16 · Shore reset: the boat drifting all the way back to the shore returns the attempt to its starting conditions, everyone aboard, sober-ish, full stamina · why: the floor the pillar No death needs, the bottom of the mountain; my reading of "at the beginning they recover and rest up", confirmed by the human · from: Q6
- 2026-10-16 · Hazard set for the first lake: floating log, angry geese guarding goslings (they attack), rock or buoy, fishing line, motorboat wake, wind gust, harsh-wind stretches, cross-current stripe, jet-ski; hazards are meant to combine, a wake or gust pushing the boat into something else · why: the human's call; variety of telegraphs and penalties, and the wind makes stamina management matter · from: Q3
- 2026-10-16 · Effects palette: beer overboard, shove, snag a paddle, stop the boat, force a stamina break (unused for now) · why: the human's call; a shared vocabulary so every hazard reads the same way · from: Q3
- 2026-10-16 · Placement: seeded bands from shore to island, weighted per band, density rising toward the island, one seed per attempt · why: reproducible for testing, varied between attempts, readable as a place · from: Q3
- 2026-10-16 · Water aboard is a hazard effect: the boat takes on water and the guy on the side it came in bails instead of paddling until it is out; the broadside wake uses it · why: the human's call; another way to lose a paddler without losing a guy · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Production order: functionality first, then pixel art and animation with every telegraph animated before decorative art, then audio · why: the human's call; the telegraphs are what make the game fair, so they come before polish · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Audio telegraphs hazards before they are visible (engine before the wake, jet-ski from offscreen, geese hissing, wind rising), then stroke, splash and guy noises · why: the human's call; sound can warn earlier than the look-ahead camera · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · Winning plays a cutscene: the boys step onto the island, make camp and drink more; the crossing ends there · why: the human's call; the payoff is the party · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · A fourth antic: needing to pee. The seat gives no strokes while he pees, and a hazard that rocks the boat hard enough knocks him in, a swimmer without a beer · why: the human's call; beer has consequences, and it links antics to hazards · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · The top drunkenness tier is a timing bar, not scrambled keys: a bar sweeps while the key is held, release in the thin green zone or the guy paddles backwards (replaces the scrambled-key decision from Q4) · why: the human's call; a skill test the player can see beats a remap they must remember · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · A missed stroke still burns stamina · why: he swung; drunk guys tire for nothing · from: conversation
- 2026-10-16 · The shore reset fires on any shore contact once the boat has left (phase CROSSING), crew aboard or not; never at launch · why: the GDD's "with or without anyone aboard", made precise so the launch shore doesn't loop · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · One lake seed per attempt: a shore reset replays the same hazard layout; a new seed only comes with a new attempt · why: the lake is learnable like Getting Over It's mountain; matches "one seed per attempt" · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The lake is a big static map the boat moves through; objects (logs, rocks, buoys, reeds, flock, dinghy, stripes) are fixed and events (wake, gust, harsh wind, jet-ski, geese charge) happen as the boat passes; only beers and swimmers drift · why: the human's first play: the world shouldn't move around the boat · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The boat drifts back slowly when nobody paddles; loose things drift noticeably faster (refines the constant current) · why: keeps the pressure and the rescue cascade without the boat feeling swept · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Placement stays seeded bands, with a guaranteed winding corridor no object is placed in · why: replayable and still always passable · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · A clean crossing takes five minutes or more; the lake is huge · why: the human wants an epic crossing · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · A beer overboard bobs away for two or three seconds while the guy visibly winds up before jumping · why: telegraphs the setback; the player can see it coming · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · A guy who falls in some other way while winding up for a beer swims for that beer anyway · why: the human's call; the beer is still his · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · An on-screen arrow points to every guy in the water who is off-screen, so the player can find him · why: the human's call; a rescue you can't locate isn't fair · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · At most one beer overboard per couple of seconds; hits inside the window still shove or stop the boat but cost no beer · why: the human's call; prevents a wipeout from a cluster of hits · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · With all four in the water the boat is uncontrollable and its drift home speeds up to about five times the headwind · why: the human's call; a lost crossing shouldn't keep the player waiting · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The control scheme is shown as text written in the sand on the launch shore, visible at the start and gone once the boat has left · why: the human's call; teaches without a HUD element during play · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The Loch-Brez beast: a lake monster that rises near the lake edges, past the island's far side, and rarely in the deep middle; it rams the boat and grabs a guy, who is spat out far back toward the shore as a swimmer, never eaten for good · why: the human's hazard, made to fit No death, only setbacks (the human chose the setback over the exception) · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The cross-current stripe stays lake-wide; the path runs through it · why: crossing a sideways push is the challenge · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Once the geese hiss the charge always comes (replaces the builder's calming rule) · why: the human's call · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The jet-ski crosses on a diagonal from the lake edge, not a horizontal line · why: the human's call · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Confirmed as built: a stroke's turn scales with its power; the sand text reappears whenever the boat is back at the launch shore · why: the human kept the builders' readings · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Eight keys, two per seat, forward and reverse: Q/A and W/S on the left hull, I/K and O/L on the right (replaces the four-key Q A I K layout); a reverse stroke pushes less than a forward one but turns and burns the same · why: the human's call; reverse paddling makes steering and backing off hazards possible · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · A timing-bar miss flips the stroke's direction (forward key → the weaker reverse stroke, reverse key → forward) · why: with reverse keys the miss needs a direction rule; the weaker push follows the reverse-stroke decision · from: conversation (agent reading, recorded)
- 2026-10-17 · The beast's random visits come from the attempt's lake seed, not the crew's RNG; a ram that can't catch the boat gives up after a timeout · why: same lake, same beast; a chase must end · from: conversation (agent reading from the T50 review, recorded)
- 2026-10-17 · Touch controls: the screen halves are the hulls, the upper and lower parts of a half the front and back seats; hold charges, release strokes forward, a flick down before release strokes in reverse; shown automatically on touchscreens · why: the human's call after research; two thumbs make choosing and alternating guys the touch difficulty, so the pillar holds · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Mobile web (phones and tablets through itch.io) joins the platforms; touch is built next, before the function check · why: the human's call · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The name: Paddla Kanot Som En Idiot; the game is also a tech demo for Godot Director, and its setting is the author's annual canoe-camping trip with friends · why: the human's background for the page · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The guys speak Swedish; later each telegraph gets a speech bubble in which the guy announces what he is about to do · why: the human's call; the warning doubles as the joke · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Key columns: Q/A left back, W/S left front, I/K right front, O/L right back (replaces the Q/A-front layout): outer keys are the back seats, inner keys the front · why: the human's call; the hands mirror each other · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The control principle is outer steers, inner propels: on keys the outer columns are the back seats; on touch each screen half splits sideways, outer part back seat, inner part front seat (replaces the seat-by-height touch layout) · why: the human's call; one layout to learn for both inputs · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Hazard warnings are also called out in Swedish by one guy aboard ("Nu jävlar blåser det!", "Se upp för fåglarna!") · why: the human's call; the telegraph becomes a character moment · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · A floating swimmer starts swimming slowly back toward the boat after a few seconds (replaces "never swims back on his own") · why: the human's call after play; a patient rescue shouldn't be a chase · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · The beast's random visit is super rare, less than once per crossing; the guaranteed path must never enter its edge zone · why: the human's play found it appearing far too often · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Other boats on the lake are drawn at least as large as the catamaran · why: the human's play: the motorboat and jet-ski were too small to recognise · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · A reset: R starts a new attempt; on touch a hold-to-reset corner button that can't be hit by accident · why: the human's call; testing needs it and a stuck attempt needs a way out · from: conversation
- 2026-10-17 · Drunkenness comes down: at the top of the meter a guy pukes overboard (telegraphed, seat out for a moment, a big drop); peeing gives a small drop · why: the human's call after play; the meter needs a way down so a crossing can recover · from: conversation
